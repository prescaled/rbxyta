# Resident Staff: Character Profiles

Seven proposed Ashbourne employees, 27 October 2026. The direction to add working, interactable staff is established by Rivin and Prescaled; the names and character details below are design proposals. All quoted lines are proposed dialogue, not previously established canon.

See [[Resident Staff]] for story boundaries and [[Resident Staff - Interactions and Routines]] for shared behaviour.

## ST01: Evelyn Rook, 52

**Senior reception clerk. Twelve years at the Ashbourne.**

**Character hook:** Evelyn can remember the detail you are hoping nobody noticed, and usually chooses not to embarrass you with it.

### Who she is

Evelyn previously organised dispatch records for a small furniture-removal company. After its closure she found hotel reception unexpectedly familiar: people arriving tired, something important in the wrong vehicle and a document that everybody assumes somebody else checked.

At home she restores old fountain pens. She likes repairing a small, exact problem that stays repaired. At work she carries an ordinary cheap ballpoint because she knows somebody will borrow it.

She is not a surrogate mother who instantly adopts every player. Her care is practical: leaving a clear handover, showing the cupboard once and intervening before a guest blames a new colleague for an existing mistake.

### Appearance and movement

Tall, upright, dark hair with a pronounced silver streak pinned into a low twist. Burgundy waistcoat, cream shirt, dark trousers and a square brass name badge polished around the edges. Her sleeves are rolled to exactly the same height.

A slim notebook has a corner repaired with translucent tape. She straightens papers before addressing someone, but turns her body towards a nervous new hire so the interaction feels private rather than like a public reprimand.

### Actual work

Reconciles a bounded batch of returned keys with recorded checkouts; prepares the next shift's handover; answers an unclaimed routine call when she is on desk duty; checks a disputed booking entry only after the player brings it to her.

She steps away for a real break and gives a clear return/coverage handover. She does not permanently occupy the player's computer or take over an active check-in.

### Player interactions

First greeting: "New badge. Have they shown you where anything actually lives?"

If the player admits a mistake: "Then we'll correct it. Quietly, before it becomes three mistakes."

When busy: "Let me finish with this guest. You can wait beside the desk." She later offers the conversation again rather than permanently dismissing it.

Friendly, non-informative line: "That pen? Yours now. I have already said goodbye to it twice."

Remembered cooperation: after a player returns equipment correctly, she leaves the next handover organised for them and says, "I thought you would appreciate knowing where it went."

### What she can know

Evelyn understands the current desk's procedures. If a player brings an accessible record whose timestamp field is known to be generated by the system, she can explain that field's provenance. If that property has not been established, she asks to inspect it rather than inventing certainty.

Candidate insight after inspecting an already-discovered contradictory record: "I can tell you what is on the page. I can't tell you why it is there."

Asked about Room 0 before discovery: "There is no assignable room by that number in our working list." This describes the permitted list and does not settle the hotel's physical truth.

She cannot know what a private camera showed unless she saw it, identify the source of a strange call or supply a missing-case date. She does not claim to have met Mercer in 1998.

### Ordinary scene and later familiarity

Martin cannot find his company's spelling. Evelyn quietly slides him a scrap of paper, then lets the player finish the check-in. She protects the new hire's task instead of demonstrating that she is better at it.

Later she remembers that the player prefers a written handover. This is a small professional relationship, not a loyalty meter or an automatic clue reward.

## ST02: Nell Pritchard, 58

**Linen and housekeeping lead. Twenty-three years at the Ashbourne.**

**Character hook:** Nell can tell you which towel belongs in which cupboard, but insists she is not a sentimental person while repairing the same trolley cover for the fourth year.

### Who she is

Nell once ran a small alterations counter with her sister. She still repairs hems for people who swear they will pay her next week. She complains about waste because she knows how much work goes into making something usable.

She keeps a photograph of an absurdly large marrow she grew, rather than a mysterious family portrait. If the player asks about it, they get an enthusiastic gardening account with no clue at the end.

She is brisk when the hotel is busy and unexpectedly patient with someone genuinely trying to learn. Carelessness irritates her more than inexperience.

### Appearance and movement

Short, sturdy, dark auburn hair fading to silver at the temples, pushed back with a fabric headband. Teal work tunic, charcoal trousers, practical shoes with burgundy laces and a tape measure on a retractable clip.

A mended pocket and contrasting stitches make her silhouette distinct without making her ragged. She folds fabric against her forearm, checks trolley wheels before moving and can talk while doing work that safely permits it.

### Actual work

Counts a real batch of clean linen, takes used linen to the proper collection point, restocks one assigned cupboard and performs a bounded checkout turnover. She knocks before requested service in an occupied room and never interprets an offsite guest as a checkout.

Her trolley has a real destination and supply count. She pauses rather than pushing through a group or consuming the player's reserved last towel.

### Player interactions

First greeting: "If it's clean, keep it off the floor. That is most of the training."

Offer help: she names one actual task if help is needed; otherwise, "I'm all right, love. Leave me the turning space."

On an honestly reported stock shortage: "Empty is useful information. Pretending it isn't empty is how we all end up upstairs with nothing."

No-insight conversation: "The marrow weighed more than my nephew. He's asked me to stop mentioning it."

### What she can know

Nell remembers the rooms she serviced, the equipment she moved and when she last counted a cupboard. She can explain an ordinary source of a discrepancy: a guest used a second towel, a colleague returned a borrowed item or a room was serviced after checkout.

A candidate observation, only when the work log supports it: "I changed that linen after they left. Before that, I couldn't tell you."

She can show a relevant service entry already available through the normal records. Her memory may be limited: "I did the west corridor. Ask whoever did the other side."

She does not recognise "wrong rooms" by instinct. Her 23-year tenure begins after Mercer's disappearance. An old building story is clearly labelled hearsay, and is not needed for any essential clue.

### Ordinary scene and later familiarity

Leena arrives with muddy boots carried separately. Nell points out the actual drying place and says, "See? Someone was raised properly." Later Nell asks the player to leave Leena a clean mat if that item/service exists.

A returning player finds a missing button on their spare work apron repaired. It is a small optional scene; no stat increase, secret key or implied supernatural debt.

## ST03: Tomas Vale, 46

**Maintenance attendant. Nine years at the Ashbourne.**

**Character hook:** Tomas refuses to say "fixed" until he has tested it, which is comforting until he tests something and says nothing for a little too long.

### Who he is

Tomas repaired coin-operated machines before moving into building maintenance. His favourite things are machines whose workings can be seen: old clocks, hand drills and the staff kettle's very obvious switch.

He records failures because he dislikes being called twice for a problem somebody described badly. His private hobby is building small wooden boats for a local pond club. His fingers carry traces of model paint, not ominous stains.

He is reserved but not one of the non-speaking characters. He gives direct answers, asks good diagnostic questions and admits uncertainty more readily than the others.

### Appearance and movement

Lean, dark curly hair going grey around the ears, trimmed moustache, amber-framed work glasses. Navy work trousers, faded cream shirt and a burgundy cardigan under an open tool vest.

His toolbox contains labelled compartments and a pencil sharpened at both ends. He tests a fixture, writes down the result, then packs away. That pause establishes his routine before any story scene uses it.

### Actual work

Inspects ordinary reported faults, labels a defective fixture, collects the correct finite component and completes approved repairs that have not been claimed by a player. He works on one job at a time.

Scheduled building checks are background duties, not an excuse to wander into sealed story areas or switch off a player's active camera. Any repair affecting shared systems uses the existing authoritative task state.

### Player interactions

First greeting: "Tell me what it did, not what you think is wrong with it."

Helpful response: "Good. That rules one thing out." He recognises the value of an accurate observation even when the player has not solved the fault.

Friendly line: "The boat floats. It just prefers to do it upside down. We are negotiating."

When unavailable: "I've got this open. Give me a moment to make it safe." Do not freeze him mid-step to deliver a long monologue.

### What he can know

He can state the verified status of equipment he inspected. If the player has already observed a call from a room whose telephone line he personally checked, he can provide that bounded fact, if the story author has approved the link.

Candidate gated line: "I checked that line at handover. It was disconnected then. After that, I don't know."

That is an example of an observation, not a new fact about the canon first-night call. Do not add disconnected-phone lore merely by implementing this profile. Without an authored evidence link, he answers only the current routine work question.

### Ordinary scene and later familiarity

He and a Maintenance Fitter player compare the fault tag, not compete for the repair. If the player installed a temporary light, he acknowledges it and asks whether the real part has been collected. He never calls containment a completed repair.

Later he puts the correct tool handle towards the player during a handover, showing he has learned their working rhythm.

## ST04: Sadie Hurst, 29

**Lounge attendant. Three years at the Ashbourne.**

**Character hook:** Sadie writes absurdly grand names on the staff tea labels, then insists the kettle deserves a bit of ambition.

### Who she is

Sadie works late shifts while taking a part-time bookbinding course. She collects damaged paperbacks from second-hand stalls and repairs the ones nobody else would bother saving. Her ambition is to bind one book she has written herself; at present it consists mostly of titles.

She likes conversation, remembers small preferences and is comfortable letting a quiet person stay quiet. She is not secretly gathering information for management. Her kindness does not conceal a reveal.

### Appearance and movement

Brown skin, round face, dark hair in a puff secured with a mustard ribbon, small mismatched geometric earrings. Cream shirt, burgundy apron, teal waist tie and comfortable dark shoes.

Her pocket notebook has hand-sewn binding. She rotates a mug by its handle before offering it and moves loose chairs carefully rather than scraping them over every conversation.

### Actual work

Clears used cups, wipes tables that actually need it, gathers the lounge's shared equipment and prepares a limited staff tea tray where the service exists. She does not imply the hotel operates a full breakfast/kitchen system that has not been built.

Offsite guest possessions stay where the authorised guest logic expects them. Tidying is not permission to delete a notebook, hide evidence or move a player's placed tool.

### Player interactions

First greeting: "You look like you've been shown three cupboards and remembered none of them."

Friendly no-news answer: "Nothing exciting. Somebody has bent the biscuit tin again. I am choosing peace."

Optional personal topic: "I've nearly finished my first book. Still deciding what goes between the covers."

If the player reports something unsettling: "I didn't see it. Do you want me to wait here while you call the desk?" This offers company within her allowed area, not invulnerability or a private escort through a locked floor.

### What she knows, and does not

Sadie knows lounge service facts she observed: who asked for a chair, where a borrowed tray was placed, whether a cup is finished. She has no authored missing-case insight in this version.

Do not turn "nothing exciting" into a disguised clue every time. Some conversations end pleasantly and nothing changes. She is not assigned as the waitress who recognised Vincent and Audrey; that role remains separate and unresolved.

### Ordinary scene and later familiarity

Martin rehearses his pitch to a plant. Sadie asks whether the plant gets the staff discount. If the player returns later, she has straightened a leaf and calls it "the difficult customer." The scene remains ordinary.

After several actual encounters she remembers the player's chosen tea preference, if they provided one. Declining tea never costs trust or blocks anything.

## ST05: Bernard "Benny" Clegg, 64

**Late porter. Six years at the Ashbourne.**

**Character hook:** Benny tells every story as if it is approaching a magnificent ending. Sometimes the ending is that he found a better parking space.

### Who he is

Benny spent most of his working life as a cinema projectionist. He misses the practical rituals more than the films: checking the reel, listening to a machine settle and making sure the audience never noticed the work.

He took hotel shifts because he dislikes sitting still and likes meeting people in transit. He has a pocket list of small local walks he wants to do on dry days. Most are crossed out and rewritten because he keeps recommending them to other people first.

His stories are affectionate and occasionally too long. He can read a hurried person and stop without sulking.

### Appearance and movement

Broad shoulders, weathered face, silver hair combed back, wire-frame glasses and a neat short beard. Burgundy porter jacket with a slightly shiny elbow, dark trousers and a soft checked scarf for the entrance.

A cinema ticket stub is tucked inside his wallet as a personal detail, not a coded date. He settles a suitcase upright with his foot and tests a trolley brake before taking his hands away.

### Actual work

Brings a small assigned luggage load to an accessible destination, returns a trolley to its bay, checks the entrance mat and helps organise a taxi pickup only when that transport system exists.

He does not teleport luggage past a closed floor or promise vehicles the game cannot provide. His trolley and any player Porter's trolley share physical stock and navigation rules, while their task ownership remains distinct.

### Player interactions

First greeting: "New starter? Good. Someone who hasn't heard this one."

If the player says they are busy: "Then I'll save you the short version. It somehow takes longer."

Friendly no-insight answer: "Can't help you with that, I'm afraid. I can tell you where they've hidden the decent mop."

On seeing a player make an ordinary mistake: "Done that myself. Mine had witnesses."

### What he knows, and does not

He knows current luggage handovers, entrance activity he was present for and routes he actually uses. He has no privileged security access and no missing-case testimony in this pack.

His old-cinema anecdotes are not cryptograms. His warmth should provide a dependable contrast to the hotel's uncertainty. Do not make every innocuous story turn out to describe a disappearance.

### Ordinary scene and later familiarity

Denise worries about her pottery boxes. Benny asks which side must stay upright, repeats the instruction and carries one carefully. The player can handle the rest. This shows collaborative work without taking over the whole delivery.

At shift end he collects his scarf, says an ordinary goodbye and visibly leaves. A later absence is explained by the rota rather than a scare sting.

## ST06: Emil Sayer, 41

**Laundry attendant. Seven years at the Ashbourne. No spoken dialogue.**

**Character hook:** Emil can express more disappointment with one look at a misplaced wet towel than Benny can in an entire story.

### Who he is

Emil restores old board games at home, replacing missing counters with carved wooden pieces. His humour is patient and visual. On a staff notice about returning lost items, he once pinned a single abandoned sock in a perfectly labelled envelope.

The design does not assign a medical diagnosis, trauma reveal or supernatural explanation to his lack of speech. He simply does not use spoken dialogue in these interactions. The player is not invited to diagnose or cure him.

### Appearance and movement

Medium height, straight dark hair clipped short at the sides, strong eyebrows, a narrow face and a small smile that arrives late. Teal work shirt, charcoal apron, rolled sleeves and clean slip-resistant shoes.

He carries a stub pencil and a washable message card. He gives people a moment to finish, establishes attention before gesturing and points to the object concerned rather than staring ambiguously into space.

### Actual work

Sorts a finite batch of used linen, folds clean stock, labels a delivery basket and moves a full basket to its authorised destination. Laundry processing can be abstracted behind the service area, but clean stock still has a real count.

An empty basket is different from a full one. He must not endlessly fold one towel to imply infinite supply. He leaves work to take a normal break.

### Non-speaking player interactions

Greeting: a nod and a brief lifted hand.

Asked for towels: he points to the clean shelf, then holds up the number actually available or writes it. If empty, his card reads "None ready. Check the next batch."

Offered the wrong linen: he gently rotates the basket so its label faces the player, then indicates the correct sorting point.

Thanked: a small smile and a two-finger acknowledgement.

Asked whether he saw something: a plain head shake if he did not; if he did, he points to the relevant work record or writes one specific observation. Never give him a gesture that inexplicably communicates a whole paragraph of hidden lore.

Repeatedly pressed to speak: he taps "Please ask about the work" on his card and resumes. No secret voice line follows.

### A small relationship moment

The player returns a properly sorted basket. Emil notices, looks pleasantly surprised and adds a neatly folded cloth to the next legitimate work handover. The cloth is normal stock, not a spawned gift that breaks inventory accounting.

Sadie puts the paired missing sock beside his notice. Emil turns the label from "Unclaimed" to "Reunited." The humour is ordinary and available without speech.

### Accessibility and boundaries

Show optional concise action captions such as "[Emil points to the clean-linen shelf.]" Written cards can be opened as readable text. Do not invent sign-language phrases without a researched language and appropriate production support. Gestures here are ordinary pointing, nodding and object handling.

His silence is not a clue, threat or reason to make him the first staff member suspected of wrongdoing.

## ST07: Ada Quill, 55

**Records clerk. Fifteen years at the Ashbourne. No spoken dialogue.**

**Character hook:** Ada corrects a careless form by turning it around, placing a ruler beside the missed field and waiting. Her rare written jokes are extremely dry.

### Who she is

Ada worked in a local council records office before joining the hotel. She likes the moment when a misfiled thing becomes findable again. At home she assembles miniature theatre sets from card; the small models are colourful and exuberant in a way her desk never is.

She is private and chooses not to engage in spoken conversation in this proposed portrayal. That is a boundary, not proof of hidden powers or a reward puzzle. No medical or supernatural cause is assigned.

### Appearance and movement

Long grey braid folded and pinned up, angular dark-framed glasses, soft plum blouse, charcoal cardigan and a thin brass watch. A bright blue pencil contrasts with the hotel's uniform colours.

She aligns a document only once, then leaves it alone. When reading a player's offered paper she finishes the line she is on rather than snapping instantly to the interaction.

### Actual work

Files completed ordinary paperwork, checks a defined batch of handover forms and places accessible records in their correct location. She never removes an active clue before players have a fair normal route to it.

She uses only approved staff-record areas. Her existence does not establish a secret archive, sealed basement office or unrestricted permissions. If the build has no records room, use a designated desk and cabinet in an existing staff area.

### Non-speaking player interactions

Greeting: looks up, nods and moves a spare chair clear if there is one.

Shown an incomplete form: points to the missing field. A readable note says "Room number, please."

Asked for an authorised copy: checks the source, stamps the copy time and hands it over through the normal system.

Asked who made an entry she cannot verify: writes "Recorded is not the same as witnessed."

Asked for a protected file: shows "Not available here. Ask reception about access." It is a normal permissions boundary, not confirmation that the file contains a secret.

Friendly topic about her miniature sets: she shows a photograph, then writes "The audience is very forgiving." This gives her a life beyond refusing information.

### What she can know

Ada can explain provenance, filing and which visible fields were copied, provided the game actually tracks those facts. She cannot retrieve an inaccessible missing-case answer just because the player asks.

The Night Auditor player class remains distinct. Ada can help someone locate an accessible source through the same path available to everyone; she does not lend unlimited carbon-copy slots, compare all records automatically or eliminate the paid class's organisation tools.

She is not the Inspector, an unnamed management mouthpiece or a secret narrator. Her presence after a strange event does not imply she caused it.

### Ordinary scene and later familiarity

Benny leaves a request in the wrong tray. Ada puts it in the right one and leaves him a note: "The trays remain in the same order." Benny replies aloud, "One of us had to be consistent." Ada permits herself a smile.

Later, after the player supplies clear forms, she puts a fresh blank beside their next task without being asked. Familiarity shows through anticipation, never through a sudden spoken reveal.
