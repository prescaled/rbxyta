# Case 2 — Marion “Marnie” Bell

**The woman who heard someone practising upstairs.**

Character draft saved at Rivin's request on 24 October 2026. Marnie travels alone. The disappearance year, room number, precise connection to Daniel Mercer and explanation of the sound remain open; this draft does not settle the secret of Room 0.

Marnie arrives at the Ashbourne alone, carrying a battered leather tool case and a supermarket bag containing her evening clothes. She is a **43-year-old piano tuner and repairer**, booked to restore the neglected upright in the hotel lounge.

Her first comment to reception is:

> “Someone’s been polishing it with furniture spray. Tell them to stop helping.”

### Who she is

Marnie spent nineteen years maintaining pianos in schools, pubs and seaside hotels with her father. He handled the customers; she did most of the difficult repairs. After he died, she discovered he had quietly been undercharging almost everyone.

She has spent the last eighteen months chasing invoices while people tell her what a lovely man he was.

The Ashbourne job matters because it pays properly: three days’ work, accommodation included, payment on completion. Enough to clear the overdue rent on their workshop. She intends to repair the piano, collect her money and leave.

She is excellent at restoring instruments and uncomfortable playing for an audience. If someone asks for a song, she says:

> “You wouldn’t ask a mechanic to race you.”

Privately, she plays the same unfinished piece her father used to practise. She knows exactly where he always made a mistake.

### What she looks like

- **Short, broad-shouldered, with a dark bob she cuts herself.** The right side is visibly shorter.
- Thin burgundy glasses on a cord. She pushes them onto her head to inspect small mechanisms, then searches for them.
- A bottle-green cardigan with one mismatched cream button, a pale shirt and brown corduroy trousers worn smooth at the knees.
- A rectangular silver watch worn on the inside of her wrist so it will not scratch a piano’s finish.
- A small crescent scar beside her left thumb from a snapped string. She rubs it while considering a difficult answer.
- Her tool case has her father’s initials, **A. BELL**, painted on it. She has added **& DAUGHTER** underneath in smaller, uneven lettering.

### What makes her memorable

Marnie is blunt, observant and quietly funny. She will complain about the hotel’s tea, then repair a waitress’s loose trolley wheel without mentioning it.

She has an irritating habit of correcting people’s recollections: which day something happened, what someone actually said, whether a noise came from above or next door. She trusts her own observations enough to make people feel dismissed.

That becomes a problem when the hotel starts contradicting her.

### Her stay at the Ashbourne

On her second evening, Marnie hears someone playing a short passage upstairs. They stop at the same difficult bar, restart, and make the same mistake.

The next morning, she asks who owns the other piano.

Reception tells her there is not one.

She does not jump to ghosts. She assumes someone has a recording playing. But that night the passage changes: whoever is practising finally gets the difficult bar right.

Then they start again, slower.

Marnie sits up in bed.

It is her father’s unfinished piece. **The part she has just heard was never written down.**

### The disappearance clue

Among the belongings left in her room is her repair notebook. Most entries are ordinary: parts, measurements, costs.

The final page reads:

> “Asked through the wall how he knew the ending.
> He said I taught him.”

This gives Marnie a personal reason to follow the sound. Its source, the exact circumstances of her disappearance and its connection to Mercer and Room 0 remain unresolved.
