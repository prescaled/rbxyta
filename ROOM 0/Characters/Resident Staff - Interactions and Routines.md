# Resident Staff: Interactions and Routines

Design proposal, 27 October 2026. Supports [[Resident Staff - Character Profiles]] and the established direction in [[Resident Staff]].

## All staff are interactable, including those who do not speak

Use a consistent small set of context-sensitive options:
- Greet or check in.
- Ask about an existing work problem.
- Offer or request a specific handover.
- Show a record or item the player actually has permission to use.
- Ask a personal topic already introduced, when the employee has time.
- Leave.

Do not display every option everywhere. A colleague working on an unsafe repair step is briefly unavailable, not frozen in place while a dialogue menu runs. A player can leave at any time without a social penalty.

Speaking and non-speaking employees use the same underlying interaction outcomes. Emil may point to a shelf; Ada may present a written note. Neither produces a voiced reply or a fake dialogue ellipsis that makes silence look like a bug. Captions describe what was visibly done without inventing thoughts.

## Five useful response states

**Available and friendly:** a short acknowledgement, with an optional topic. No obligation to issue a job.

**Available but uninformed:** an honest "I didn't see it" or equivalent gesture. Do not generate a clue to fill the branch.

**Busy:** acknowledge the player and finish the current safe step. Offer a natural nearby waiting place or invite them back. No mandatory wait for casual conversation.

**Able to help:** give one relevant instruction, accept a handover or provide a bounded observation from a legitimate source.

**Boundary or permission refusal:** be clear about the limit. Emil can decline personal interrogation; Ada can refuse inaccessible records; Evelyn can state the official working room list without claiming it is the whole truth.

An employee who cannot help may know which current department handles a routine problem. Avoid making every refusal a chain of errands leading to a lore reward.

## Conversation structure and repetition

Aim for short first responses, with optional follow-ups. Proposed starter writing scope per character: one introduction, three ordinary greetings, three job-context replies, two personal exchanges, a busy response, a no-information response, a goodbye and two remembered-work variants. Silent characters need equivalent action/written responses, not an artificially smaller personality.

These are writing targets, not a promise of all lines already drafted. Use the profile's actual lines first and author missing variations before implementing them.

Rotate routine barks with a cooldown; do not repeat the same story every time a player walks past. An exhausted personal topic may receive "Nothing new since we spoke" or a friendly gesture. Do not show a literal "No more dialogue" message.

Remember small concrete events: correctly returned equipment, an admitted mistake, a fulfilled handover, a voluntarily stated tea preference. Do not invent shared history on reconnect or transfer a private player conversation to everyone else's memory.

No visible affection meter is needed for this first design. Repeated clicking, gifts or pestering silent staff cannot grind access to evidence.

## The difference between insight and revelation

Every informative reply should be authored with:
- What the player must already have observed or possess.
- What the employee actually knows and how they know it.
- The smallest useful answer they can give.
- What remains uncertain.
- A normal alternative route if the information is required for progression.

A practical answer can precede a mystery without being a prediction. Tomas showing how he tests a fixture establishes his credibility. Later, if a specific story scene is approved, his record of having tested it can matter.

Optional comments enrich interpretation. Essential information cannot depend solely on finding one employee during a narrow rota window. If an important staff observation is approved, preserve it in an accessible authorised work log or allow a later conversation. Do not add a replacement document with more knowledge than the employee had.

For the first-night empty-room call, staff can react only to the facts the player has discovered. This pack does not choose which employee knows a disconnected-line fact, name the caller, settle Room 0 or explain a missing person's mechanism.

## Duties with actual outcomes

Each employee alternates between a short work route, a completed bounded action, a pause or colleague interaction, a break and a handover. Use a small library of shared behaviours with character-specific props and timing.

Examples of real bounded work:
- Evelyn processes an unclaimed returned-key batch and produces a handover entry.
- Nell takes a specified number of linen units to an allocated cupboard.
- Tomas inspects one assigned fault and leaves a valid tag.
- Sadie removes actual used cups from a table.
- Benny transfers a particular luggage load to its agreed accessible destination.
- Emil sorts one batch and updates its location/state.
- Ada files a completed non-critical form into the correct accessible collection.

Never spawn work solely so an NPC can perform it indefinitely. They can finish a job, sit down, have a harmless exchange or wait for another duty.

## Protecting player responsibility

Divide work into three explicit kinds:

**Staff background duties:** small real jobs outside the player's current objective allocation. These make the workplace credible without silently completing the whole shift.

**Shared duties:** a task with one authoritative owner/reservation and an explicit handover. The player may offer help. Work already performed and consumed stock remain attached to the same task.

**Player duties:** onboarding tasks, active claimed jobs, story interactions and selected requests. Staff do not finish these offscreen or take them while the player fetches the required item.

NPC staff must use finite stock and return loaned equipment. Before taking the last supply, check existing task reservations. If unavailable, they wait or report the shortage; they do not conjure stock.

When a player requests help, the employee can accept a small eligible step if available, name the limit or politely decline because of existing work. "Do everything for me" is never an option. Basic assistance is available to all player classes.

A proposed starting budget is no more than one shared-task assist per employee per shift, with normal background duties handled separately. This value is only for initial tests. Essential onboarding explanations and narrative facts must not consume that assistance budget.

## Credit and inspection

Player contribution records only the work the player actually performed. Do not award a player's full payout for an NPC finishing their task. Preserve legitimate player transport/inspection/help credit when work is handed over.

The hotel's real cleanliness and guest experience can reflect NPC work, but the player's completion denominator must include only objectives allocated to players or formally accepted as shared work. Otherwise staff either inflate player scores or make players look idle by completing tasks they never had.

NPC staff do not collect player coins, trigger private class bonuses or act as purchasable class replacements. The Night Manager class can request cooperation through normal options, not command obedience.

## Proposed shift rhythm

Use relative phases until the actual shift duration is fixed. These are cast plans, not canonical clock times.

**Handover and early shift:** Evelyn introduces desk procedures; Nell handles linen movement; Sadie tends the lounge. Emil appears for a short basket handover. New hires have real work to do, and staff teach one relevant step rather than dumping a tutorial.

**Main service period:** two or three continue regular duties while one takes a visible break. Tomas or Benny can rotate in as their systems become available. Avoid sending all workers through the same narrow doorway at once.

**Quiet period:** the hotel is less busy but not artificially abandoned. One regular remains accessible at the desk or through a documented work location, and another has a visible service route. Staff on break have a normal break destination. A staff member being off duty is not a disappearance.

**Closing and morning handover:** outgoing staff pack equipment, write a handover or say goodbye. Ada may arrive for the record batch, or appear earlier if scheduled; she is separate from the Inspector's inspection.

Do not make all seven work every phase of every shift. Set the night's roster, breaks and ordinary departures before play; do not reshuffle employees into empty rooms merely to surprise the player.

## Four example encounters

**A new colleague, not an incompetent player.** Evelyn offers to show where returned keys go. A player who already understands can decline. She accepts it and lets them work. No forced mistake is authored to justify her presence.

**The wrong basket.** A player brings clean linen to the used-linen point. Emil turns the label towards them and gestures to the clean shelf. Nell, if nearby, says, "He has saved us both a conversation." The player corrects the handover; there is no penalty for not reading a gesture without captions.

**An ordinary break.** Sadie asks whether the player wants tea. Benny begins a cinema anecdote. The player may stay, decline or leave mid-story to answer a call. The conversation grants no secret object and does not deliberately punish social play with an unavoidable missed deadline.

**A relevant contradiction.** The player shows Evelyn an already-discovered questionable record. She states only what she can verify about it. Ada, if legitimately involved in filing that source, can show its copy time in writing. Tomas contributes only if his actual work log relates to it. These are possible interaction patterns, not three new mandatory clues or proof of a supernatural explanation.

## Staff-to-staff relationships

- Evelyn trusts Nell's handovers but asks her to write down changes rather than rely on everyone remembering.
- Nell and Emil work comfortably without speech. Neither treats him as absent when talking nearby.
- Sadie can make Tomas laugh by misnaming the staff kettle, but stops joking when he is handling a real fault.
- Benny tells Ada his stories anyway, then asks whether she wants the rest. Her written "Tomorrow" is a boundary he respects.
- Ada and Evelyn disagree about whether a form needs a second copy. Their disagreement is ordinary bureaucracy, not evidence of a conspiracy.

Relationships should appear through short exchanges and behaviour, not an exposition scene explaining all seven friendships.

## Silence, readability and player respect

Never use silence itself as a horror cue. A non-speaking employee can be kind, annoyed, amused or busy without an eerie sound, forced close-up or prediction that they are hiding something.

Written notes must be legible on phone and controller layouts. Offer text equivalents for small cards and captions for important gestures. No colour-only instructions or required hearing. A player can complete the same work with audio muted.

No quest markers over guests or staff; discover interaction prompts at reasonable proximity using the existing game's interaction language. Do not add hold-to-talk progress bars. Keep conversation prompts distinct from physical pickup/repair controls.

## Persistence and multiplayer

Store only needed facts: staff roster, current duty and task ownership, finite carried stock, relevant work observations, conversational topics offered and player-specific familiarity events.

Several players may greet an employee, but only one exclusive physical handover can own an item at a time. Queue or decline additional handovers clearly. Avoid making one player's open dialogue panel freeze an NPC for the entire server.

On disconnect, release a conversation and recover held task reservations safely without duplicating items. Preserve accepted shared-task work. An employee can resume their duty from a safe step; do not teleport through a locked boundary.

Staff do not share omniscient knowledge. A concern told to Evelyn reaches another employee only through an authored or logged handover. Refusing small talk cannot reduce later access to required information.

## Production and acceptance

No implementation is claimed by these notes. Reuse existing staff props and shared rigs under [[Art Bible]] and claim any future modelling/code work through [[Work Board]].

Start with four characters and one real work route each. Add a greeting, busy state, valid handover, no-information state, ordinary departure and small remembered event before writing a large branching conversation tree.

Before calling the staff system ready, verify:
1. Each staff member performs a real bounded duty and can visibly finish or pause it.
2. Every employee can be approached, including the two with no speech.
3. Emil and Ada have zero voiced replies and clear accessible responses.
4. Sadie and Benny can complete conversations without clues, rewards or ominous hooks.
5. Insight replies require legitimate knowledge and cannot reveal author-only case notes.
6. An NPC cannot consume a reserved item, steal an active player task or duplicate stock.
7. Solo and multiplayer shifts remain completable without mandatory staff composition.
8. Ordinary departures and breaks are accounted for.
9. Essential information has a fair later/alternative route.
10. The new cast does not replace the Inspector, unnamed management, the earlier New Employee or the Case 3 waitress.
11. Player contribution is not awarded for work exclusively performed by NPCs.
12. Conversational rejection, repeated questions and disconnects do not create a softlock.

Open decisions for later: final names/appearance approval, exact rota, which current locations support each duty, assistance budget, required story links, whether any staff observation ever becomes mandatory evidence, and the production quality of gestures/captions.
