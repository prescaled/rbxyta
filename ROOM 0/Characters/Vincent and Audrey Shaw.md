# Case 3 — Vincent and Audrey Shaw

**The couple who checked out before they arrived.**

Character and story direction approved for these notes by Rivin on 24 October 2026. Two guests travel together, share one room and both disappear. Case 3 is one disappearance case involving two people, not a third individual guest. The disappearance year, room number, links to the other cases and the hotel's underlying mechanism remain open.

### Who they are

Vincent, **56**, and Audrey, **54**, have been married for twenty-nine years. For the last eleven months, they have lived in separate houses on the same street.

They have not told their daughter.

Now she is getting married nearby, and they have booked one room at the Ashbourne to keep up appearances over the wedding weekend. Audrey considers this a practical arrangement. Vincent has brought the tie she bought him for their tenth anniversary.

Neither mentions it.

They arrive together, argue quietly about who has the reservation, and then give reception exactly the same answer at exactly the same time.

### Audrey Shaw

Audrey runs a small alterations shop. She can look at someone across a room and tell where their jacket was taken in badly. While talking, she straightens crooked cuffs and folds paper into precise rectangles.

She has a **silver-blonde bob, heavy-lidded eyes and a small gap between her front teeth** that only shows when she laughs properly. She wears a plum trouser suit, low black heels and an enamel kingfisher brooch. Her handbag contains sewing scissors, boiled sweets and receipts going back several years.

She is dry, direct and difficult to embarrass. If a waiter asks whether they are celebrating anything, she answers:

> “Successfully finding the car park.”

Audrey left because Vincent could turn any serious conversation into a joke. She still finds him funny. That annoys her more than anything.

### Vincent Shaw

Vincent used to demonstrate kitchen appliances at department stores. He can make a roomful of strangers interested in a vegetable peeler. In private, he struggles to finish a sentence that actually matters.

He is **tall, soft around the middle, with thinning copper hair combed carefully sideways**. His navy suit fits badly at the shoulders because Audrey has not altered it. He carries a disposable camera and wears polished brown shoes with one replacement lace that does not quite match.

He remembers everyone’s tea order. He forgets appointments. He calls Audrey “Aud” without thinking and corrects himself when she notices.

At reception, he asks for two keys.

> Vincent: “We’re very independent people.”
> Audrey, taking hers: “Recently.”

### What makes them feel like a real pair

Their separation has not erased their habits.

Vincent passes Audrey his plate before she asks for the tomatoes he dislikes. Audrey checks that he has his reading glasses before they leave the room. When they argue, their voices get quieter.

They know how to look after each other. They have forgotten how to explain what they need.

When something goes wrong, each is still the person the other reaches for.

### What happens at the Ashbourne

At check-in, reception finds a completed bill under their names.

It records **two occupants**, their correct home address and a departure time of **6:40 that morning**.

Audrey assumes somebody has duplicated the booking. Vincent jokes that he hopes they enjoyed their stay.

Then Audrey sees the signature.

It is hers—including the small line she draws through the unused space beside a total so nobody can add another charge.

She insists she has never been to the hotel.

Later, a waitress recognises them.

She says they sat at breakfast that morning. Vincent had tomatoes on his plate. Audrey was crying.

When Audrey asks what they were talking about, the waitress hesitates.

> “You asked him to promise he wouldn’t leave you upstairs again.”

### Their disappearance

That night, Vincent receives a call from reception saying Audrey is waiting downstairs.

Audrey is standing beside him.

They go down together to sort it out. Before leaving, Audrey takes his hand—an automatic gesture neither comments on.

**Neither returns.**

The next morning, their room contains both suitcases, their wedding clothes and their daughter’s unopened card.

On the desk is a second completed bill.

Same names. Same room. Two occupants. Departure: **6:40 a.m.**

This time, Vincent has signed it.

Under his signature, in Audrey’s handwriting:

> **“We left together this time.”**

Two distinct people entered the Ashbourne, and both are missing.

### AUTHOR-ONLY EXPLANATION — Rivin and Prescaled

**Writing reference / spoilers. Do not present this explanation as guest dialogue or an audience-facing reveal by default. “Author-only” is a content label, not an access restriction on the board or repository.**

The hotel has already forced Vincent and Audrey through an earlier version of their stay, which neither remembers when they arrive in the version the audience follows. The first completed bill and the waitress’s recollection are evidence of that earlier stay.

#### What “again” means

In the earlier version, Vincent followed a false message downstairs alone, believing Audrey was already there. The hotel separated them: Audrey remained upstairs while he searched for her.

They eventually reached breakfast together, but she remembered being abandoned and he could not explain where he had been. That is the conversation the waitress recalls when she says:

> “You asked him to promise he wouldn’t leave you upstairs again.”

“Again” refers to that earlier separation inside the hotel. It is not simply a reference to their marital separation.

#### What “this time” means

During the version we follow, the false message arrives while Audrey is visibly beside Vincent. They recognise the contradiction and go downstairs holding hands.

They avoid being separated, but **staying together does not get them out of the hotel. Both disappear.**

Audrey’s final message means:

> “We didn’t let it separate us again.”

It **does not confirm that they escaped**. The hotel’s bill calls it a checkout; their actual destination remains unknown.

#### Continuity to preserve

- Earlier stay: separated by the false message, then reunited before the breakfast the waitress remembers.
- Arrival we follow: neither consciously remembers the earlier stay.
- Later that night: the false message is exposed because Audrey is physically beside Vincent.
- Final outcome: they remain together, and both become missing.
- Audrey’s final handwriting suggests some memory of the earlier stay returned before she wrote it. The exact moment of that return remains undecided.
- Do not imply that a real receptionist knowingly made the false call; its source remains unresolved.
- The fixed emotional outcome is that this time they choose each other. The hotel’s mechanism, the exact handling of the repeated stay and their whereabouts remain open.
