# Resident Staff

Detailed staff-cast proposal for ROOM 0, 27 October 2026. Start at [[NPC Character Notes]].

## What the player should feel

The Ashbourne had a working life before the player arrived. Somebody knows which drawer sticks, who returns borrowed equipment and when to put the kettle on. New staff enter an existing workplace rather than an empty hotel that only starts moving when they accept a task.

Staff recognise the player through ordinary cooperation. A first-night "You're the new one" can become "I left the trolley clear for you." Familiarity should come from a completed handover or remembered interaction, not a visible friendship grind.

These employees are people with shifts, tired feet, preferences and boundaries. They can be reassuring without being infallible. An employee can have nothing important to say and still be worth visiting.

## Seven proposed regulars

- **ST01 Evelyn Rook, 52, senior reception clerk.** Composed, meticulous and quietly protective. Knows how the current desk records are made; careful about distinguishing a caller's claim from a verified fact. Practical insight and occasional evidence-based observations.
- **ST02 Nell Pritchard, 58, linen and housekeeping lead.** Dry humour, strong standards and a habit of keeping repairable things. Knows rooms through servicing and stock through touch. Practical spatial knowledge, never automatic anomaly detection.
- **ST03 Tomas Vale, 46, maintenance attendant.** Patient, exact and reluctant to offer a comforting guess. Knows what ordinary equipment can and cannot do. Helps interpret faults without becoming an oracle for supernatural causes.
- **ST04 Sadie Hurst, 29, lounge attendant.** Warm, playful and studying bookbinding in her spare time. Keeps the lounge working and gives the player ordinary company. No assigned missing-case secret.
- **ST05 Bernard "Benny" Clegg, 64, late porter.** Affable former cinema projectionist who enjoys telling a story properly, even when nothing dramatic happened. Carries luggage and eases awkward arrivals. No hidden clue behind his rambling anecdotes.
- **ST06 Emil Sayer, 41, laundry attendant.** Does not speak in the proposed game interactions. Patient, attentive and precise; responds through gestures and brief written practical messages. Silence is not hostility or a horror diagnosis.
- **ST07 Ada Quill, 55, records clerk.** Does not speak in the proposed game interactions. Formal, wry and firm about privacy; communicates through written replies, pointing and offering documents the player may legitimately see. Has clerical knowledge but no automatic access to the hotel's secret.

## Information is grounded, not evenly distributed

Evelyn, Nell and Tomas can offer insight because of their jobs. Their insight usually helps ordinary work first. Examples: how a desk entry is created, which towel trolley serviced a room, or whether a particular light was actually disconnected.

They must have a source for what they know: an observed event, a record they handled or an established work procedure. No staff member senses an anomaly from an internal game flag.

Sadie and Benny are deliberately allowed to remain ordinary. Do not later fill every empty dialogue branch with ominous warnings. The player should be able to enjoy a harmless conversation without being trained to interrogate everyone until a secret drops.

Emil and Ada communicate without spoken lines. Useful replies may be written or gestural; both still have greetings, acknowledgements, refusals and task handovers. Asking them to speak repeatedly never unlocks a secret voice line or earns a reward.

## Narrative boundaries

- The seven regulars remain accounted for in this initial design. They leave through ordinary shift changes. This pack adds no fourth disappearance.
- Their current tenure does not establish a missing-case date. Evelyn has worked here 12 years, Nell 23, Tomas 9, Sadie 3, Benny 6, Emil 7 and Ada 15. In a 2026 setting, none is being presented as an Ashbourne employee in 1998.
- They may discuss Mercer only from evidence or history already legitimately available. Do not invent eyewitness accounts.
- Marnie's year and the Shaws' year are unresolved. Do not identify Sadie as the waitress who recognised the Shaws or decide that any regular employee knew Marnie personally.
- Hotel Management's refusal to acknowledge Room 0 remains the institutional position. Staff can separate official instructions from personal observations without confirming management's motive.
- Ada is not the Inspector. Evelyn is not the unnamed manager. None is declared to be the earlier New Employee.
- The author-only meaning of "again" and "this time" remains author-only unless an explicit story reveal is separately written.
- Their personal backstories are writing references, not a wall of facts the player learns at first greeting.

## Recommended first group

Build Evelyn, Nell, Sadie and Emil first. This gives reception, physical housekeeping, friendly conversation and one fully non-speaking interaction pattern without requiring advanced records or maintenance AI immediately.

Add Tomas when fault inspection/repair is reliable; Benny when luggage and trolley navigation are reliable; Ada when accessible records and copying permissions are reliable. If the lounge or laundry route does not exist, stage their introduction in a functioning staff area instead of inventing a connected room.

Seven profiles form a content roster, not a demand that all seven appear every night. Aim for three or four present during a typical phase of a shift, with one or two visible handovers. Start smaller if navigation or performance requires it.

## The balance with players

NPC staff do real bounded work. They also have their own workload and cannot cover the whole hotel. Staff actions use finite supplies, have visible results and respect ownership of tasks.

Players keep meaningful responsibility for the core shift. Staff should never finish the player's first check-in, take the repair out of their hands or solve a report offscreen. Equally, "background work" should not mean an employee mimes polishing a clean glass forever. Give them believable small completed jobs.

When a new hire chooses the Night Manager class, that is a gameplay role, not permission to command NPCs, change their history or outrank the established institution automatically.
