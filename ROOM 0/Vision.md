# Vision (original pitch)

The original ROOM 0 pitch, kept as written (reference image links removed). Summarised and linked from [[ROOM 0 - Home|ROOM 0]].

---

Imagine scrolling through TikTok late at night.

You come across a video. It isn't someone playing Roblox. It isn't an advertisement. It looks like a piece of security footage from an old hotel.

A narrator begins:

"In 1998, a guest checked into the Ashbourne Hotel. His name was Daniel Mercer. He was assigned Room 214. Three days later, the room was found empty. His belongings were still there. The security cameras showed him entering his room, but never leaving."

The footage cuts to a hotel corridor.

"Twenty-eight years later, a new employee discovered something strange. Daniel Mercer was still listed as a guest. His room number had changed."

The camera zooms into an old computer monitor.

ROOM 0 — OCCUPIED.

The video ends.

No explanation. Just a name: ROOM 0.

People begin asking questions. Who is Daniel Mercer? What happened to him? Why does a room numbered zero exist?

Then another video appears. Another guest. Another strange recording. A connection to the previous disappearance.

Slowly, an audience begins discovering the story of a hotel that should not exist.

And eventually, they discover something even more interesting.

They can enter the hotel themselves.

## The world we're building

ROOM 0 is a multiplayer psychological mystery game on Roblox, set inside the fictional Ashbourne Hotel.

From the outside, the Ashbourne is an ordinary hotel. Guests arrive, employees work their shifts, rooms are cleaned, and everything appears normal.

But the hotel has a history.

Guests have disappeared. Employees have reported corridors changing overnight. Certain rooms appear on security cameras but cannot be found on the building's floor plans.

And somewhere inside the hotel is Room 0.

Nobody knows exactly where it is.

Nobody knows why it exists.

And the hotel's management refuses to acknowledge it.

The central mystery is simple:

What is Room 0, and why does the hotel seem determined to keep it hidden?

The answer is something we would develop carefully, long before revealing it to players. We don't want random mysteries with no explanation. We want a coherent fictional universe where small details eventually connect.

## What players actually do

Players take the role of newly hired night-shift employees at the Ashbourne Hotel.

They can enter with friends or play alone.

Their first shift seems ordinary. They check guests in, deliver requested items, investigate maintenance problems, monitor security cameras and respond to calls from rooms.

But as the night progresses, unusual things begin happening.

A guest calls from a room that is supposed to be empty.

A security camera shows someone standing behind a player, even though the corridor is empty.

An employee receives instructions from management that contradict something another player has discovered.

The players must investigate these events while continuing their work.

Every shift presents different incidents, clues and decisions. Some events are relatively harmless. Others lead players deeper into the hotel's history.

The objective isn't simply to survive until morning. It's to complete the shift, uncover evidence and gradually understand the hotel's secrets.

As players progress, they unlock restricted areas, discover documents and piece together information about previous guests.

Eventually, they may discover how to access Room 0.

But finding the room is only the beginning.

## The experience we want players to have

I want ROOM 0 to create three distinct feelings.

Curiosity. Players should constantly question what they see. A small detail in a guest's room might become important several shifts later.

Uncertainty. Not everything strange should be dangerous. Players should have to investigate rather than immediately run away whenever something unusual happens.

Discovery. Players should feel rewarded for paying attention. A clue mentioned in a TikTok might correspond to an actual object hidden somewhere inside the game.

This is what connects our content strategy to the game.

Someone watching our videos could recognise a room number, a name, a symbol or a particular date.

When they enter the game, they might discover that the detail actually matters.

That creates a much stronger connection between the audience and the game than simply telling viewers to join.

## Our content will tell the story before the game launches

**The Missing Guests.** Short fictional documentary-style videos about people who disappeared from the Ashbourne Hotel. Each introduces characters, dates and clues that eventually connect.

**The Security Archives.** Strange recordings supposedly recovered from the hotel's surveillance system. Viewers might notice details that the narrator never mentions.

**The Employee Files.** Internal hotel documents, staff training recordings and unusual workplace instructions. These introduce mechanics and rules players will encounter in the game.

**The Discoveries.** Eventually, we introduce actual Roblox footage showing that viewers can explore the Ashbourne themselves. The story becomes interactive.

The videos should look like carefully produced pieces of fiction, not generic AI slideshows.

We can use ElevenLabs for consistent character voices, AI image generation for cinematic scenes, and editing to create a recognisable visual identity.

Importantly, the footage should be presented as part of a fictional story rather than genuine real-world evidence.

## How the project develops

1. **Phase 1 — Establish the mystery.** We create the Ashbourne Hotel's history, its main characters, the central secret and the visual identity of ROOM 0. We produce our first pilot videos and test whether people are interested in the mystery.
2. **Phase 2 — Build the first shift.** We develop one detailed hotel floor, a reception area, basic employee tasks and several mysterious events. This becomes our first playable prototype.
3. **Phase 3 — Connect the stories.** We release more Shorts while adding discoveries to the game. Viewers begin recognising details shared between the videos and the hotel.
4. **Phase 4 — Open the hotel.** Once the experience is stable and genuinely enjoyable, we launch a public version. Our videos begin directing interested viewers to investigate the hotel themselves.
5. **Phase 5 — Expand the universe.** We introduce new hotel areas, guest histories, mysteries and story chapters. The world grows through both gameplay and short-form storytelling.

## The long-term ambition

I don't want ROOM 0 to depend on one viral video.

I want it to become a recognisable fictional world.

A world where viewers recognise the hotel's name before they've ever played the game.

Where people discuss theories about characters and unexplained events.

Where friends enter Roblox together because they've seen something online and want to investigate it.

And where the game itself gives us more stories to tell.

But to achieve that, we'll have to be disciplined. We're two people, not a large development studio. Our first version should focus on one excellent gameplay loop, one memorable location and a handful of genuinely interesting mysteries.

We can expand once we've demonstrated that people enjoy both the storytelling and the gameplay.

## The principle behind ROOM 0

Every room has a number.
Every guest has a record.
Every disappearance has an explanation.
Except Room 0.

That's the identity I would build around.

Not a Roblox game with some AI videos attached.

A fictional mystery told across TikTok, YouTube and Roblox, where watching the story makes you want to play, and playing makes you want to discover more of the story.

Our next major creative decision should be defining the actual secret behind Room 0. Once we know the truth, we can work backwards to create clues, characters, plot twists and gameplay that all connect rather than inventing mysteries as we go.
