# Ordinary Guests: Group Bookings

Eight proposed bookings: four trios and four quartets. Every member is an adult in this initial catalogue. A family-with-children booking can be designed separately later with appropriate room arrangements and supervision; it is not needed to make the first roster varied.

Use approved triple or four-person rooms with suitable separate sleeping spaces. Each group has one booking and a stable named roster, but individuals have different activities and needs.

## T01 · Nia Brooks, 28; Beth Lane, 29; Callum Ross, 30 · The wedding guests

**Visit and relationships:** two nights for a friend's wedding offsite. Nia and Beth are old friends; Callum became part of the group later and is the calmest organiser.

**Look:** Nia has a neat dark bob, purple travel jacket and a garment bag; Beth has copper curls, trainers and a floral dress carrier; Callum wears rectangular glasses and carries a suit on a hanger plus a tiny sewing kit.

**Personalities:** Nia has the schedule saved in detail. Beth remembers people and forgets objects. Callum claims not to care about photographs, then carefully fixes everyone's collars.

**Arrival:** Nia handles the booking, Beth studies the venue directions and Callum watches the bags. Their attention shifts when Beth realises her shoes are packed inside somebody else's case.

**Routine:** arrive, settle, borrow an iron if needed, get ready, gather at the lobby mirror and leave for the wedding. Later they can return separately in two waves while remaining registered guests.

**Request choices:** iron; extra hangers; a taxi confirmation. One shared equipment request serves the group. Return the iron before leaving or arrange an agreed collection.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Beth asks, “Do we look coordinated?” Callum replies, “We look like we've met. That's enough.”

**Room and departure:** tissue paper, clothing bags and a misplaced earring found before checkout. They check the room together and leave with all three people accounted for.

**Variation:** Beth is unusually organised on a later visit, which the others notice. No wedding guest vanishes and no empty dress becomes a horror cue.

## T02 · Trevor Moss, 52; Eli Moss, 24; Dev Patel, 25 · The away-match supporters

**Visit and relationships:** one or two nights for an offsite sporting event. Trevor is Eli's father; Dev is Eli's friend. All three are adults.

**Look:** Trevor wears a faded team scarf and sensible waterproof; Eli has a modern cap and patched backpack; Dev has a pale green jacket and brings an overlarge packet of snacks.

**Personalities:** Trevor recalls every old match but gets modern transport details wrong. Eli wants to stay on schedule. Dev enjoys the trip even if the team loses.

**Arrival:** Trevor begins booking under an old confirmation name. Eli supplies the current details without making a scene. Dev is already checking when they need to leave.

**Routine:** review travel plans, go out to the event, return discussing one disputed moment and settle in the lounge if noise levels permit. Their disappointment never justifies abuse.

**Request choices:** transport information; an extra towel after rain; a wake-up call. They should not require the hotel to provide live sports broadcasting.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Trevor says, “I've seen worse.” Dev asks, “Is that meant to help?”

**Room and departure:** folded scarves, snack packaging and wet coats in the agreed place. They conduct a final ticket-and-wallet check and leave for home.

**Variation:** win or lose changes conversation and energy, not their basic courtesy or the certainty of a normal departure.

## T03 · June Harper, 60; Leon Harper, 58; Paula Voss, 59 · The amateur history weekend

**Visit and relationships:** two nights for a public local history event. June and Leon are siblings; Paula is their longstanding friend. They are interested in ordinary regional history, not investigators of the missing cases.

**Look:** June wears a tan coat and carries labelled folders; Leon has brushed-back grey hair and a pocket sketchbook; Paula wears bright blue reading glasses and has the lightest bag.

**Personalities:** June collects exact dates. Leon draws buildings and forgets times. Paula remembers what people said and spots when a long discussion needs to end.

**Arrival:** June asks about public photographs on display. They accept a polite “we don't know” and do not demand access to closed rooms or staff files.

**Routine:** visit the offsite event, compare notes over tea and sketch a public lobby feature without obstructing passage. Separate quiet activities precede an evening regroup.

**Request choices:** reading light; extra paper; clarification of a public opening time. No invented historical answer should be treated as canon.

**Small scene and dialogue:** June: “It could be late Victorian.” Paula: “The chair is occupied, whatever period it is.”

**Room and departure:** pamphlets, a neatly folded map and sketching materials taken away. They ask permission before leaving a benign sketch as thanks.

**Variation:** different event topics generate new everyday conversation. They do not accidentally solve Room 0.

## T04 · Ines Duarte, 33; Patrick Osei, 36; Rowan Hale, 32 · The small trade-fair team

**Visit and relationships:** three nights while presenting their small stationery business at an offsite trade fair. Colleagues sharing a triple room by prior agreement.

**Look:** Ines wears a navy jumpsuit with rolled sleeves; Patrick has round glasses and a cream polo; Rowan wears a moss-green jumper and carries a labelled crate of sample notebooks.

**Personalities:** Ines makes decisions quickly. Patrick enjoys people but needs quiet after a day of selling. Rowan designs the samples and worries that nobody will notice the paper quality.

**Arrival:** confirm beds, breakfast arrangements if offered and receipt details. Only one speaks to reception at a time.

**Routine:** leave together, return carrying a lighter sample crate, reconcile orders at a table, then split into rest, a phone call and quiet reading.

**Request choices:** packing tape if stocked; working desk light; an additional night after a confirmed follow-up appointment. The extension needs capacity and a genuine itinerary change.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Rowan offers everyone a notebook. Patrick says, “Please let somebody pay for one tomorrow.”

**Room and departure:** labelled packing sheets and reusable crates, no overflowing public display. They count stock, settle the bill and leave.

**Variation:** one successful order or an unexpectedly quiet day affects their conversation. Their small business outcome is not determined entirely by hotel service.

## Q01 · Dawn Keating, 44; Pete Nolan, 51; Ivo Novak, 37; Kelly Watts, 26 · The road crew

**Visit and relationships:** three nights during an offsite maintenance contract. Four colleagues sharing a properly equipped room.

**Look:** Dawn has a compact braid and weathered blue work jacket; Pete wears a knitted cap and has a large lunch tin; Ivo has a close beard and meticulous boots; Kelly has short curls and a bright orange water bottle. Work gear is cleaned or removed before public seating.

**Personalities:** Dawn coordinates logistics. Pete tells long stories. Ivo quietly gets everyone organised. Kelly is capable but still learning when the older three are joking.

**Arrival:** Dawn checks the booking while the others keep bags together. Confirm four sleeping spaces and the actual early-departure plan.

**Routine:** early departures, tired returns, washing, dinner if available, a brief lounge conversation and early sleep. One member may collect a group order while the others rest.

**Request choices:** four towels as one delivery; drying arrangements; repair of one genuinely noisy fixture. They do not automatically generate four times the work.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Pete begins a story. Ivo says, “We have heard the bridge one.” Pete replies, “This is the other bridge.”

**Room and departure:** work clothes contained, paired boots, one shared rubbish bag. They carry out their own heavy equipment and leave in a normal regrouped party.

**Variation:** a contract extension can justify an extra night if a suitable room is free.

## Q02 · Mina Clarke, 38; Jo Bennett, 40; Tess Morgan, 37; Ben Okoro, 39 · The friends who kept the annual weekend

**Visit and relationships:** two leisure nights. Four friends who rarely get everyone in the same place.

**Look:** Mina has a bright checked tote; Jo wears a dark cardigan with rolled sleeves; Tess has long hair in a loose knot and a paperback full of markers; Ben wears an oversized cream jumper and carries a board-game box.

**Personalities:** Mina plans. Jo happily negotiates. Tess needs quiet intervals. Ben remembers everyone's favourite snack and insists he has simplified the game rules.

**Arrival:** greet one another as much as reception. Two arrive a little earlier and wait for the others before completing the shared check-in.

**Routine:** ordinary offsite outing, a lounge game at an available table, separate breaks for reading or calls and an agreed quiet hour. They are capable of enjoying silence together.

**Request choices:** permission to use an available table; enough cups for a shared drink order; an extra blanket for one person. Never assume game use grants exclusive ownership of the lounge.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Ben: “The rules are quite simple.” Tess closes her book: “You said that forty minutes ago.”

**Room and departure:** game pieces counted back into their box, snack packaging and neatly separated luggage. They take a departure photograph and leave normally.

**Variation:** change the game or who wins. Their friendship continues offscreen; not every visit needs an emotional revelation.

## Q03 · Farah Ahmed, 42; Michael Webb, 45; Tessa King, 41; Len Frost, 57 · The conference volunteers

**Visit and relationships:** two nights for an offsite charity conference. Four volunteers who know each other through event work and have agreed to share accommodation.

**Look:** Farah has a burgundy blouse and a colour-coded folder; Michael wears a soft grey jacket and has a box of name badges; Tessa has short blonde hair and a reusable coffee cup; Len has a checked shirt and a pen clipped at an impractical angle.

**Personalities:** Farah sees dependencies. Michael remembers names. Tessa cuts through unnecessary meetings. Len quietly repairs minor presentation props but asks before using hotel equipment.

**Arrival:** confirm the payer and receipt once. A postponed check-in is handled calmly if the room is still being cleaned.

**Routine:** label badges at an available table, go to the conference, return to debrief and then stop talking about work when Tessa insists.

**Request choices:** spare pens if stocked; a delayed arrival note for one member; a reliable morning call. Printing and office services are optional later features, never assumed to exist.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Tessa: “We are off duty.” Len: “These badges won't alphabetise themselves.” Michael: “They already have. Twice.”

**Room and departure:** sorted badge boxes and papers, all confidential event material packed away. They check nothing has been left in public space, pay and depart.

**Variation:** attendance numbers change their mood, but the hotel is not responsible for the event's success.

## Q04 · Alice Marsh, 49; Sofia Marsh, 22; Marcus Marsh, 24; Neil Marsh, 47 · The family on a stopover

**Visit and relationships:** one night during a longer ordinary journey. Alice and Neil are married; Sofia and Marcus are their adult children. Four-person family room with an appropriate bed arrangement.

**Look:** Alice has a green cross-body bag and practical short hair; Neil wears a brown fleece and carries a paper route printout; Sofia wears a denim jacket and bright headphones; Marcus has a striped rugby shirt and a large snack bag.

**Personalities:** Alice wants everyone settled. Neil wants tomorrow's departure agreed. Sofia likes a little time alone. Marcus is sociable but keeps borrowing other people's charging leads.

**Arrival:** confirm the shared booking. Sofia reads the signs herself while Marcus asks a question already answered by one.

**Routine:** stretch after the journey, eat or go out, make separate calls, meet briefly in the lounge, then sleep. The adult children are free to spend time independently.

**Request choices:** a suitable extra pillow; a replacement towel; a wake-up call or transport question. Charging equipment is supplied only if the hotel actually stocks compatible loaners.

**Small scene and dialogue:** Alice: “Has everybody got everything?” Marcus: “Define everything.” Sofia hands him his jacket.

**Room and departure:** snack crumbs, several cups and carefully packed luggage. A final bag count takes longer than paying the bill, then all four leave.

**Variation:** a planned onward stop may change; they still depart through ordinary arrangements and never become a missing-family plot.
