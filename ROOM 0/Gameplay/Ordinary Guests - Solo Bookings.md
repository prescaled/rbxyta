# Ordinary Guests: Solo Bookings

Six proposed bookings. All leave normally. Durations describe intended stays, not fixed in-game minute lengths. Requests are alternatives unless described as a sequence; do not trigger every listed request on every visit.

## S01 · Martin Pike, 47 · The salesman who rehearses with the furniture

**Visit:** one night before demonstrating commercial coffee machines at an offsite supplier meeting. Optional second night only if another appointment is added.

**Look:** sandy hair flattened by rain, navy jacket with an old lapel pin, practical shoes and a wheeled case with one squeaking wheel. A sample brochure has been folded into his breast pocket like a handkerchief.

**Personality:** cheerful in a practiced way, anxious about silence. He remembers names immediately but sometimes repeats his own introduction. Away from customers, he is softer-spoken.

**Arrival:** carefully asks for a receipt in the company name, then discovers the correct spelling is on a crumpled note inside his glasses case. He lets the next person speak while he finds it.

**Routine:** checks his paperwork, uses a lounge chair with a table, whispers the opening lines of his presentation, takes a phone call outside the conversation area and packs before bed. On the next morning he is ready before his taxi.

**Possible requests:** a wake-up call; a working reading lamp; help with a stuck luggage wheel only as a later optional interaction. The lamp request ends when the lamp works, after which he actually reads.

**Small scene:** after a successful stay, he confidently presents his brochure to a plant, catches himself and says, “That one was for tomorrow.”

**Room trace and departure:** aligned brochures, a tea-ringed coaster and a used tissue from polishing his shoes. He checks under the bed for a sample, thanks reception by name and leaves with the case.

**Dialogue:** “Not a quiet room exactly. A room where I won't be the loudest thing.”

**Return variation:** his presentation went badly or well; he shares one sentence about it. Neither outcome changes whether he pays or checks out normally.

## S02 · Leena Shah, 31 · The surveyor with a private bird list

**Visit:** two nights for an offsite building survey. A weather delay can justify one extra night.

**Look:** cropped dark hair, amber glasses, a rust waterproof jacket and clean indoor shoes carried separately from muddy boots. Her field notebook has careful measurements on the front pages and birds drawn at the back.

**Personality:** observant, economical with words, unexpectedly delighted by small things. She is precise about time but loses track of it when something interests her.

**Arrival:** asks where wet boots can go before carrying them farther inside. Takes a room away from late lobby traffic if one is available.

**Routine:** leaves with a packed bag, returns damp, cleans her glasses, sketches in the lounge and phones a colleague to discuss tomorrow's route. A window seat is preferred, not mandatory.

**Possible requests:** a drying space or towels after rain; confirmation of an early breakfast option if that service exists; a replacement room bulb. Never promise a breakfast service that has not been designed.

**Small scene:** she stops mid-sentence to watch a bird outside, then apologises: “Sorry. That was a better appointment.”

**Room trace and departure:** a folded waterproof map, neatly paired boots and one muddy towel in the correct place. Her notebook stays in her possession. She makes a final weather check and leaves for the next survey.

**Dialogue:** “The rain isn't the problem. It's pretending it will stop.”

**Return variation:** she recognises a changed plant near reception or has added a bird to the back of her notebook. This is an ordinary observation, not a hidden clue.

## S03 · Denise Holt, 62 · The novice exhibitor

**Visit:** two nights while entering a small regional craft fair. She has made ceramic mugs for years but this is the first time she has paid for a stall.

**Look:** broad silver braid, denim overshirt, turquoise earrings and boxes marked HANDLE THIS ONE MORE CAREFULLY. One hand has a small patch of dry clay near a fingernail.

**Personality:** generous with practical advice, evasive about her own nerves. She calls the fair “a little outing” while carefully checking every price label.

**Arrival:** wants to know where she can safely keep packed stock. She does not unpack fragile goods in a busy doorway.

**Routine:** sorts labels at a table, asks another willing guest which of two prices looks clearer, attends the fair offsite and returns with fewer boxes or the same boxes rearranged.

**Possible requests:** an extra blanket to cushion a box; a quiet table if one is available; directions for her collection vehicle. Carrying stock is optional, not an unavoidable heavy task.

**Small scene:** rehearses saying the full price of a mug without apologising. After the fair she allows herself one pleased smile before announcing that parking was terrible.

**Room trace and departure:** paper wrapping gathered into one bag, a pencil stub and stacked empty boxes. She counts the boxes aloud at the exit.

**Dialogue:** “They are handmade. I'm trying to stop saying that like it's a defect.”

**Return variation:** she may have sold one favourite item, or decided to try again despite a poor day. A gifted mug is a rare authored thank-you, not a payment obligation.

## S04 · Omar Benton, 28 · The interview candidate

**Visit:** one night before an interview at a nearby organisation. His occupation and interview result remain ordinary background, not a progression gate.

**Look:** close-cut hair, new grey suit, trainers for travelling and formal shoes in a cloth bag. A small grooming comb protrudes from his document wallet.

**Personality:** considerate, over-prepared, prone to apologising before asking a reasonable question. When relaxed, he is much funnier than his rehearsed answers.

**Arrival:** has the reservation reference written in three places. Asks once where the exit and reception will be in the morning, then repeats the route under his breath.

**Routine:** practises answers quietly, irons a shirt if equipment is supplied, checks the weather and turns in early. His restless return to the lounge is to calm down, not an anomaly.

**Possible requests:** an iron or shirt pressing option; a wake-up call with a confirmed time; clarification about transport. The same equipment must not be promised simultaneously to multiple bookings.

**Small scene:** begins a casual conversation with “My greatest weakness is…” and stops. Later he laughs about it himself.

**Room trace and departure:** a carefully hung empty garment bag and practice notes taken away or deliberately binned. He leaves with all documents and both pairs of shoes.

**Dialogue:** “Could you tell me if this tie says reliable or assistant headteacher?”

**Return variation:** a later visit may reveal he got the job, found a different one or is interviewing again. Do not award success solely because a hotel request was completed.

## S05 · Ruth Calder, 69 · The crossword competitor

**Visit:** one or two nights for an offsite puzzle club gathering. She likes the hotel because its lounge is quieter than the event venue.

**Look:** short white curls, a mustard cardigan, dark straight trousers and a flat canvas bag full of newspapers. Uses a small magnifier when the print is poor.

**Personality:** mischievous and highly competitive, but a gracious loser once she has had a minute. She enjoys company without demanding it.

**Arrival:** asks whether the lounge closes. Checks that the chair she chooses has enough light rather than claiming it for the whole stay.

**Routine:** reads, completes crosswords, invites an available guest to debate a clue, takes a short walk and puts her paper away when somebody needs the table.

**Possible requests:** a brighter reading position; a spare pen; help locating a dropped glasses case. Her favourite pen is never genuinely supernatural or a mandatory collectible.

**Small scene:** confidently supplies a wrong answer, realises it and turns the newspaper around as if a different angle might help.

**Room trace and departure:** finished papers stacked for recycling, biscuit crumbs on one saucer and a bookmark inside her own book. She waves with the rolled newspaper as she leaves.

**Dialogue:** “I don't cheat. I conduct discussions.”

**Return variation:** remembers one previous argument about a clue; does not recite the same joke every encounter.

## S06 · Felix Ward, 39 · The delivery driver on a proper rest stop

**Visit:** one night between scheduled deliveries. He has already finished driving for the evening and wants an undisturbed rest.

**Look:** dark curls, a fleece with an unremarkable courier logo, pale lines where gloves cover his wrists and a compact holdall. He carries a reusable flask with several replaced lids.

**Personality:** initially quiet from fatigue, patient with people and impatient with rattling equipment. He is not grumpy by default.

**Arrival:** asks about the available evening meal or nearby alternative and where to park as permitted. He never needs to drive an exhausted character into danger for a storyline.

**Routine:** eats if service is open, calls his partner, fills his flask where permitted, goes to bed and leaves early. One brief lounge visit is enough.

**Possible requests:** a room away from a loud group; correction of a rattling vent; an early wake-up call. Resolve the actual noise instead of treating his complaint as arbitrary.

**Small scene:** automatically checks the label on his own bag before picking it up, then smiles at himself.

**Room trace and departure:** minimal mess, curtains left closed and towels together. He checks his route, collects his flask and leaves visibly.

**Dialogue:** “A quiet room and a kettle that works. That's my luxury package.”

**Return variation:** gradually exchanges familiar greetings. Some stays have no requests at all; reliable rest is his successful small story.
