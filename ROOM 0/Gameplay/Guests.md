# Guests

Guests give the Ashbourne an ordinary daily life: arrivals, routines, requests, relationships and departures. They create [[Shift Tasks]] and contribute to the [[End of Shift Inspection]], but also have things to do when nobody is serving them.

## Established direction

- **A different number of guests checks in every night.** Some nights are quiet, some are busy.
- **Bookings contain 1–4 guests sharing a suitable room.** Rivin retained the original maximum of four after considering three.
- **Guests can stay for several nights.** Good service can encourage an extended stay.
- **Groups have distinct members.** Couples, relatives, friends and colleagues share a booking but have individual habits and needs.
- **The current expanded catalogue is for guests who do not disappear.** Their temporary outings, sleeping and separate returns are ordinary states.
- The three [[Missing Guests]] cases remain separate story material. Anomalous visitors in [[Incidents]] are also separate from these ordinary bookings.

## Stay cycle

1. Arrive alone or as a party and check in.
2. Receive an appropriate room, with a suitable bed arrangement for every occupant.
3. Settle, use public spaces, rest, go out or spend time with companions.
4. Make occasional context-driven requests rather than an endless chain.
5. Remember relevant service outcomes during the stay.
6. Stay to the planned departure or request an eligible extension.
7. Pack, settle the bill, return keys and depart normally.
8. Turn over the room before another booking uses it.

## Current tuning proposal

These starting values replace older ambiguous or automatic assumptions; none are locked before playtesting.

| Rule | Proposed starting point | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| New arrivals | 2–4 new bookings in a small opening test | Only when suitable rooms and active population allow; continuing residents count |
| Party size mix | 40% solo, 30% pair, 20% trio, 10% quartet | Per booking, averaging two people; not a guaranteed mix every night |
| Stay length | Usually 1–3 nights, initial cap 4 | Extensions require itinerary flexibility and room capacity |
| Service requests | Typically 0–2 per booking per night | Alternatives, not a requirement to trigger all available requests |
| Active requests | Initially one per booking and three hotel-wide | Tune against shift length and desired pace |
| Satisfaction | Existing 0–100-per-room idea remains a possible summary | Detailed weights undecided; individual interactions should remain visible |

The earlier “3–8 new guests per night” line did not distinguish people from bookings and is superseded for this proposal. The earlier automatic “+1 night whenever happy” becomes a conditional extension; happy guests with fixed travel plans still leave normally.

## Personality and request variety

Regular, demanding and night-owl remain possible traits, not rigid NPC classes. Group is a booking size rather than a personality. Guests can be considerate, nervous, sociable, private, organised or distracted in different combinations.

Requests arise from circumstances: wet clothing, a wedding, an early appointment, a faulty light or a comfort preference. Supplying four towels to a quartet is one group request, not four separate interruptions.
