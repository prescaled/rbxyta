# Cinematics: Scene Catalogue

Twelve candidate scenes for Rivin and Prescaled, 27 October 2026. See [[Cinematics - Direction and Pacing]] for shared limits. Durations and night gates are proposed. "Trace" below means physical aftermath; an optional saved observation is different.

## S01: The Window at Arrival

**Format:** chapter cinematic, about 20-25 seconds. First-shift arrival; subtle only.

**Sequence:** the staff shuttle approaches through rain; warm hotel windows become visible. At one upper window a distant silhouette closes a curtain. The shuttle turns, taking the window out of view. On arrival, the player is handed back normal control at the entrance.

**Why it works:** at this stage there is nothing demonstrably supernatural. The hotel feels occupied before the new hires arrive. Do not have the silhouette race across floors or point directly at the camera.

**Aftermath:** no clue or report ticket. The window is simply a window. In a repeat arrival, the curtain may already be closed; absence is not a missed objective.

**Dependencies:** approved shuttle sequence and safe entry handoff. This elaborates the existing Arrival proposal; it is not a separate scene that must play immediately afterward.

## S02: The Service Crossing

**Format:** world sighting, 2-4 seconds. Nights 2+.

**Trigger:** an eligible player approaches a corridor junction during ordinary work, with a clear view across a short doorway opening and no active danger.

**Sequence:** a dark human-shaped figure crosses the opening rapidly, moving across the player's view rather than rushing at their face. Two soft footfalls continue behind the wall. When the player reaches the junction, the route is empty and the expected exit door is still in its prior state.

**Variation:** one version uses only the lower legs beneath a partly lowered service shutter if that prop exists. Another leaves a brief coat edge visible behind a column. Use one, not both.

**Aftermath:** no dropped item, changed guest roster or mandatory search. Looking away does not fail a task. This is an atmospheric apparition, not a reportable timed enemy.

**Dependencies:** a real occluder and validated route. No teleport through an open doorway in full view. This is the simplest prototype of the user's "zooms past" idea.

## S03: Someone Behind You

**Format:** world/feed event, about 8-12 seconds. Nights 2+; paired with C02.

**Trigger:** a camera operator watches an eligible colleague in a corridor. There is space behind the colleague and no real NPC occupying the staged position.

**Sequence:** an indistinct figure becomes visible on the feed several steps behind the colleague. It approaches once while the colleague continues ordinary work. The colleague turns: their physical corridor is empty. The figure on the feed is gone by the time they complete the turn.

**Co-op payoff:** the person at the desk has a reason to say "Behind you," while their colleague has a reasonable reason not to believe them. No forced camera turn and no jump to every player's POV.

**Aftermath:** no physical trace. A frame captured while the figure was present remains valid. If nobody captures it, use a non-punitive sighting log, not a failed anomaly ticket.

**Solo:** a separate authored variant uses the security-entry feed, with a figure outside the player's current room. Do not show the solo player inexplicably standing in a distant corridor.

**Dependencies:** camera-only rendering and consistent evidence. Develops an existing canon incident, not a new predator attack.

## S04: The Green Cardigan

**Format:** world sighting, 2-3 seconds. Later, after the Marnie case identity is introduced and narrative permission exists.

**Sequence:** the player glimpses a short figure in a green cardigan crossing a distant opening with a battered rectangular case. The uneven bob and case silhouette are readable for a moment. The figure disappears behind a wall; no continuation appears on the other side.

**Sound:** one case-latch click, not a new melody that explains the unfinished piano piece.

**Aftermath:** no physical item, voice explaining her fate or confirmation that this is literally Marnie. A journal entry can describe "a figure resembling the woman in the case record" only if the player has unlocked that record.

**Variation:** a second authored staging uses the same silhouette in the far edge of the lounge glass. Select one staging for a run; do not repeatedly parade her model.

**Dependencies:** approved Marnie appearance and case gates. Do not supply an unapproved disappearance year or room number.

## S05: Two Guests, One Empty Landing

**Format:** interactive discovery or world sighting, 6-10 seconds. Shaw case known; later narrative approval.

**Sequence:** two figures stand together on a landing, one in plum, one in a poorly fitting navy suit. One takes the other's hand. They move behind a large landing column as the player approaches. There is no one beyond it.

**Composition:** the camera or player can read that two people were together. Do not split one off, leave a single apparition behind or imply that the pair avoided the established disappearance.

**Aftermath:** the landing is empty. No wedding card materialises and neither suitcase becomes loot. A saved observation can preserve the paired silhouette without explaining why it occurred.

**Why it works:** the gesture is recognisable to someone who knows Vincent and Audrey, while the impossible absence remains unresolved.

**Dependencies:** a valid occluding landing, approved pair assets and narrative gate. No "again" or "this time" explanation appears as a subtitle.

## S06: The Reflection Arrives First

**Format:** world sighting, 5-8 seconds. Nights 2-3+, non-damaging.

**Sequence:** while passing a glazed lobby door or fixed decorative mirror, the player sees a figure approaching in the reflection. The visible physical corridor alongside it is empty. A normal door closer swings across the reflected sightline. When the glass becomes clear again, the figure is absent.

**Player agency:** they can turn immediately or keep looking. Never force them to stare at the glass until the effect completes.

**Aftermath:** no handprint unless a separately authored variant explicitly needs it. This version leaves no physical evidence.

**Variation:** the figure is stationary in the glass while the player's position changes. Do not combine both reflection rules within one encounter.

**Dependencies:** fixed-angle reflection staging or a validated render technique. If the implementation cannot preserve believable angles, postpone it rather than calling a visible duplicate model a reflection.

## S07: The Lift That Did Not Deliver

**Format:** world sighting, about 7 seconds. Nights 2+.

**Sequence:** the lift arrival bell sounds. Through the narrowing gap of a closing pair of doors, a dark figure is briefly visible inside, facing the rear wall. The player can press the normal reopen control. When the doors reopen, the car is empty.

**Fairness:** this is an idle lift with no actual occupant or active player trip. Never trap a player in a fake travel sequence, move them to an unapproved floor or close doors on a group to stage the scare.

**Aftermath:** no person appears in the arrival register. The lift resumes its normal usable state.

**Variation:** a later variant shows the hem of a coat at the back of the car. Do not add a sudden face lunging forward.

**Dependencies:** working safe lift-door states. A static lift facade is insufficient if the player is offered a real reopen button.

## S08: The Coat on the Hook

**Format:** world reveal, 3-6 seconds. Low-intensity, can be an early false alarm.

**Sequence:** warm light casts the silhouette of a tall person through a staff-room doorway. On approach, the player discovers a hanging coat and a mop handle. Benny or another legitimately nearby colleague may later collect the coat through an ordinary routine.

**Aftermath:** there was no apparition and nothing to report. The object placement remains understandable after the reveal. A correctly described ordinary object does not become an anomaly just because it was frightening.

**Why include it:** trustworthy ordinary explanations make the next real contradiction stronger. The player learns that investigation can bring relief.

**Dependencies:** lighting and props, not a new NPC rig. Do not place the coat on Emil or Ada to make their silence seem sinister.

## S09: The Last Step

**Format:** world/audio event, 4-7 seconds. Nights 2+.

**Sequence:** while the player walks a safe quiet corridor, another set of footsteps follows at an uneven offset. The player stops; one final footstep lands behind them. Turning reveals no one.

**Presentation:** no actual approaching actor is required in this version. A caption can indicate "[A footstep behind you.]" without identifying a person. It must not use a teammate's live voice or imply that a real guest has disappeared.

**Aftermath:** no damage, physical trace or report penalty. If another player or employee is walking nearby, defer the event so the authored sound is not confused with a real pathing bug.

**Variation:** soft case wheels stop one beat late. Treat this as a different sound staging, not proof of Mercer's or Marnie's possessions.

**Dependencies:** positional audio and accessible captions. This should remain effective without extreme volume.

## S10: The Reception Glass

**Format:** interactive discovery, 8-12 seconds. Later, after the relevant case reference is available.

**Sequence:** the player finishes a legitimate record inspection. In the glass beyond the desk, a stationary human silhouette is visible near the entrance. The player exits the desk view voluntarily. The entrance is empty. Returning to the record does not replay the moment.

**Identity:** use a generic figure initially. A Mercer-specific version is deferred until his appearance is approved. The record already listing Mercer is established lore; this scene must not invent his face or show a confirmed resurrection.

**Aftermath:** retain the actual record the player was reading, but add no new signed bill or hidden entry. The silhouette is a separate visual observation.

**Dependencies:** safe transition out of the desk task. Preserve any in-progress selections and never consume a key, payment or form submission during the scare.

## S11: The Return That Never Reached the Door

**Format:** short world/feed sequence, 8-12 seconds. Later, anonymous figure; relates to C08.

**Sequence:** a dark figure is visible at the far end of a covered passage. It walks towards the entrance, passes behind one pillar and never reaches the next opening. The route has no authorised side exit between those two visible points.

**Contrast:** it moves at an ordinary pace. The disturbing element is the missing continuation, not speed.

**Aftermath:** no new room assignment and no failed rescue objective. If observed only through a camera, any saved frame is labelled with its actual capture time; it does not establish where the figure went.

**Dependencies:** a fixed comprehensible route with clear occlusion. A real staff member must not be retasked into the same position mid-event. No required clue depends on catching the exact pillar transition.

## S12: Dawn, with One Unfinished Look

**Format:** chapter closing scene, 12-20 seconds. Inspection transition; selected nights only.

**Sequence:** the hotel becomes quieter as daylight rises. Staff complete visible handovers; the Inspector enters through the ordinary inspection route. Near the end of the scene, a distant upper-window silhouette is visible for a few seconds. A passing architectural obstruction hides it as the scene ends.

**Restraint:** no shout, no final attack and no Inspector dialogue confirming the silhouette. On many shifts there is no figure at all. Relief is allowed to remain relief.

**Aftermath:** normal payout and progression proceed. Skipping the shot cannot lose a reward, clue or guest outcome.

**Dependencies:** correct Inspector timing and safely settled shift state. This develops Dawn and Inspection; it does not add a second payout scene or establish that the Inspector controls the apparition.

## Selecting a first set

Start with S02, S03 and S08 for three different feelings: sudden sighting, interpersonal uncertainty and an ordinary explanation. S04 and S05 are stronger narrative moments held for approved case progression. S06 is visually promising but technically riskier; prototype it after simpler occlusion works.

Sighting actors are not automatically killable. The existing predator combat system remains separate. If a later story turns a specific apparition into a hostile entity, it needs its own readable transition, rules and approval.
