# Ordinary Guests: Pair Bookings

Six proposed two-person bookings. Each person has an identity and independent behaviour. Bed type is confirmed before assigning a shared room. All departures are normal, including early departures or separately timed journeys.

## P01 · Gwen Ellis, 66, and Arthur Ellis, 68 · The anniversary surprise nobody can hide

**Visit:** two nights for their fortieth anniversary, with a possible extra leisure night. They are affectionate and comfortable together; their story is distinct from Vincent and Audrey Shaw's strained marriage.

**Look:** Gwen has a blunt grey fringe, a cobalt scarf and a small compact camera. Arthur wears a checked jacket and carries flowers in increasingly battered paper.

**Individuality:** Gwen likes schedules and pretends she has not noticed surprises. Arthur is enthusiastic but cannot whisper effectively. She chooses the outing; he wants one romantic gesture to work.

**Arrival:** Arthur tries to arrange flowers while Gwen is still within hearing distance. She examines a wall photograph with exaggerated concentration.

**Routine:** walk out together, return to compare photographs, dress for an offsite dinner and read in neighbouring lounge chairs. Gwen sometimes goes upstairs first while Arthur finishes a conversation.

**Requests:** a vase; a recommendation for an ordinary outing; an extra pillow. A vase request can be fulfilled with a suitable jug if Gwen agrees. Flowers should not create an endless task chain.

**Small scene:** Arthur asks a receptionist to keep “the special thing” secret. Gwen passes behind him and quietly supplies the correct dinner time.

**Room and departure:** gift wrapping, a handwritten card and two books. They pack together, collect the flowers and leave a brief thanks.

**Dialogue:** Gwen: “I'm going to look over there until he finishes being subtle.” Arthur: “It's a surprise.” Gwen: “It has been since Tuesday.”

**Return variation:** Arthur remembers which vessel held the flowers. Gwen may request the same room only if it is available, not evict another booking.

## P02 · Aisha Reed, 46, and Tom Reed, 19 · The first visit to somewhere new

**Visit:** one night before an offsite college open day. Mother and adult son share a twin room.

**Look:** Aisha has tightly pinned hair, a practical green coat and an overfilled tote. Tom has loose curls, a faded astronomy sweatshirt and a backpack with a broken zip pull.

**Individuality:** Aisha asks every practical question because she is nervous about him leaving home. Tom wants to handle the booking himself but privately asks her to check his alarm.

**Arrival:** Aisha starts answering. Tom gently interrupts, reads out the reservation and gets one detail wrong. She waits until he looks at her before helping.

**Routine:** compare the route, eat or go out, split briefly while Tom explores public areas and Aisha calls home, then meet in the lounge. Tom is interested in the hotel notice board rather than forbidden doors.

**Requests:** a twin-bed confirmation; an early call; a second towel or help with the room lamp. Keep the parent-child dynamic affectionate rather than humiliating.

**Small scene:** Aisha pretends not to notice Tom taking a photograph to send her even though they are in the same building.

**Room and departure:** open-day papers, snacks and bags packed at different speeds. They do a door-side check for phone, wallet and charger, then leave for the event.

**Dialogue:** Tom: “I've got it.” Aisha: “I know. I'm checking whether you've got all of it.”

**Return variation:** months later they might return for another visit, with Tom now taking the lead confidently. This is optional continuity, not a new mandatory university location.

## P03 · Saira Khan, 34, and Mel Porter, 35 · Walking partners with incompatible definitions of easy

**Visit:** two nights for walking routes outside the hotel. A third night is possible if both choose a different route.

**Look:** Saira wears a teal shell jacket and carefully maintained boots; Mel wears a red fleece and carries more snacks than equipment. Both have ordinary weather-appropriate bags.

**Individuality:** Saira loves planning and underestimates how long photography takes. Mel complains theatrically about hills but notices beautiful details Saira walks past.

**Arrival:** ask about safe public routes and weather information. The design does not promise a playable outdoor hiking system; their walk can happen offsite.

**Routine:** spread a map on a lounge table, leave together, return at different walking speeds but meet at reception, clean footwear and trade photographs.

**Requests:** towels or a drying spot; hot drinks if available; a room radiator fault. Wet boots are kept away from other guests' bags.

**Small scene:** Mel announces they have discovered “an excellent flat walk.” Saira points out it is the route to the dining room.

**Room and departure:** drying socks, neatly folded maps and a surprisingly large snack wrapper bag. They repack all equipment and collect any item left drying.

**Dialogue:** Mel: “You said a gentle incline.” Saira: “It was gentle compared with the next one.”

**Return variation:** swap who chooses the route. Sometimes they spend the entire rainy day reading with no requests beyond a drink.

## P04 · Colin Price, 58, and Bea Price, 55 · The siblings selling the same stock twice

**Visit:** two nights for an antiques market. Brother and sister share a twin room to reduce business costs.

**Look:** Colin has a brushed moustache, waistcoat and magnifying eyepiece; Bea wears a charcoal jumper, round red glasses and an old leather satchel with an excellent calculator.

**Individuality:** Colin tells elaborate stories about objects. Bea keeps track of what they actually paid. Neither is dishonest; she trims his flourishes down to facts.

**Arrival:** negotiate where packed stock may be stored without blocking a public area. Valuable stock remains their responsibility.

**Routine:** compare inventory in the lounge, go to market, return to reconcile sales and debate whether a chipped teapot was a good purchase.

**Requests:** a receipt correction; packing material if stocked; a suitable luggage holding option. Do not turn handling their valuables into a required theft or loss event.

**Small scene:** Colin begins describing an object's “extraordinary provenance.” Bea quietly says, “Tuesday. Car boot sale.”

**Room and departure:** wrapping paper contained in a box, two mugs and inventory sheets. They count containers independently, disagree, recount and finally leave with the same number.

**Dialogue:** Bea: “We are allowed to buy things. We are also meant to sell some.”

**Return variation:** bring a different small prop and remember a previous practical favour. Their stock is not automatically linked to Room 0.

## P05 · Rosa Finch, 27, and Kit Doyle, 29 · The touring performers on a quiet night

**Visit:** one night between small offsite performances. A second night is possible if a booking changes. Friends sharing a twin room.

**Look:** Rosa has shaved sides beneath a long dark fringe and a padded instrument case; Kit wears a loose brown shirt and has a small case of cables carefully tied with coloured loops.

**Individuality:** Rosa is confident on stage and shy in casual conversation. Kit handles logistics and cannot resist checking whether a chair rocks.

**Arrival:** ask about instrument storage and the quietest way to carry cases upstairs. Neither expects a performance space or free rehearsal room.

**Routine:** eat, check equipment silently, phone the next venue and rest. If a lounge conversation happens, it is short and at ordinary speaking volume.

**Requests:** an additional chair if the room can safely fit it; a working socket for ordinary equipment; a delayed meal only if service allows it. No unauthorised rewiring.

**Small scene:** Kit can identify their own cable instantly but keeps picking up Rosa's scarf by mistake.

**Room and departure:** coiled leads, a handwritten set list and neatly packed cases. They make a two-person equipment count at the exit.

**Dialogue:** Rosa: “No, we don't need to play anything. That is rather the attraction tonight.”

**Return variation:** a familiar greeting after a successful show or a quiet evening after a difficult one. Their instruments do not repeat Marnie's supernatural motif.

## P06 · Joyce Bellamy, 71, and Val Norton, 70 · The friends who finally booked the trip

**Visit:** three leisure nights. They have discussed a weekend away for years and now refuse to spend it rushing.

**Look:** Joyce wears bright square glasses and a tidy yellow raincoat; Val has swept-back silver hair, a navy cardigan and a small sketchbook. Twin beds requested.

**Individuality:** Joyce collects leaflets and wants options. Val wants a good chair and time to draw. They enjoy negotiating a day that includes both.

**Arrival:** Joyce asks several questions, writes none of the answers down and immediately asks Val what was said. Val has already noted the useful part.

**Routine:** leisurely breakfast where available, an offsite outing, sketching or postcards in the lounge, then an early evening. Each can spend time alone without the other being “lost.”

**Requests:** better reading light; extra hangers; confirmation of a taxi time. A room change is a rare meaningful event, not their default behaviour.

**Small scene:** Joyce lays out six leaflets. Val places her sketchbook on five of them and says, “Now we can see the day.”

**Room and departure:** folded leaflets, used cups and deliberately left recyclable paper. They pack early, enjoy one last sit in the lounge and depart together.

**Dialogue:** Joyce: “We ought to make the most of it.” Val: “I am. I'm sitting down.”

**Return variation:** send an ordinary postcard or return later with one new shared plan. Their familiarity should feel earned, not immediately intimate.
